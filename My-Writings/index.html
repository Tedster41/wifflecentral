B-Side Guide

A common idiom in fiction, popularised by Stan Lee and the like, is “with great power comes great responsibility”. It’s this idea that only those who can be trusted not to use their powers for evil and self-gain should have them, and that those who gain this kind of power should feel this weight of trust put upon them. In the comics of Lee and the like, there’s usually a mix of heroes and villains who have powers. Heroes take the above quote to heart: they act in the interests of the public, saving the day whenever the day comes. The villains are the opposite: they produce situations where people require saving, where business is interrupted, where the regular is rendered ruined. They have various motives: some to mess with the heroes, some simply for the love of the game, but a villain’s motive is almost always personal gain. The bad guys are self-centered, egotistical to the nth degree, and will do whatever it takes, destroy as many buildings or lives as they must, to get what they want. This is the tried and tested dynamic: the hero, helping everyone else, and the villain, helping themselves.

The following is a collection of stories about a girl who, should she have to fit into this dynamic, would be a villain. She works for attention, for adoration, and solely for herself. She won’t take flak from anyone, and she won’t let anyone appear more powerful than her. No army, no weapon, and no person can stop her… apart from one. With every step she takes, she finds a weight on her leg. Clinging on behind her is a boy, four days younger than her, who is desperate for no one’s adoration but hers. The two are old friends, once sharing in the pre-school tools of train tracks and marble runs, but as time wore on and their friendship seemed to fall apart, something happened that would change their lives forever, which only they could truly understand. It was in this event that the girl gained the great power she felt the universe owed her, and boy what a mistake the universe had made in giving her great responsibility. Now, with a mind free of any care for anyone but the boy she seems to loathe, it is only him that can carry the torch that Lee lit, and promise the world that perhaps his best friend has some ounce of responsibility in her.
