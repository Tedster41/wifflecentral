B-Side Guide

Frankie Goes to Hollywood - Liverpool

So, remember how on the other Frankie album I said the band never released a successful album again? Well, this was their other, so-called unsuccessful album. After this they split acrimoniously. Personally, I actually think this is a better record overall, but it didn't sell nearly as well as the band's first album. Lead singer Holly Johnson argues that the poor sales were a result of the band changing sound from synth-pop to a more rock-oriented sound, with some synthesisers. Basically, that distinctly 80s "Final Countdown" style. They also switched producers; Johnson had problems with Trevor Horn, who produced their first record (and all of its insane remixes), and so this record was instead produced by Stephen Lipson. However, Lipson was Horn's engineer, so he had seen the amount of remixes that had been made for their first record. And more importantly, he realised that since this was the band's first album since then, they probably had to live up to expectations. And so he remixed away!

Let's start with the lead single: "Rage Hard". There's no need to put the 7" version on here as it's exactly the same as the album version (bar Holly Johnson talking over the intro a little), however the 12" mix, "The Young Person's Guide to the 12" Mix" (personally my favourite Frankie 12" mix), will go on. This version appeared on the first 12" release. The second 12" release featured the "Broad" mix, and the ⊕⊕⊕✪ version, a mashup of the two versions, was released on CD single, replacing the cassette singles released around the band's first album. Then, to celebrate the single debuting in the top 10, the band released the "Stamped" version on 7". Now onto the B-sides: all releases featured "(Don't Lose What's Left) Of Your Little Mind", although (naturally) only the 12" featured the full-length version. The first 12" featured a cover of Bowie's "SuffRAGEtte City" (notice the pun), while the second 12" featured a cover of the Doors' "Roadhouse Blues". The CD featured both, although the latter was presented in an edited form. Finally, both 12" releases featured a hidden track on the end, in classic Frankie style, colloquially called "Always note the sequencer…", a quote from "The Young Person's Guide to the 12" Mix".

Now, with the second single, we run into a problem. They let Trevor Horn do the remixes. …sigh, let's do this. First: the 7" single is again not counted, as the A-side, "Warriors (of the Wasteland)", is an edit of the 12" mix (I can see the argument to include it, so you can if you want), and the B-side "Warriors of the Wasteland", is the album version. As for why they're titled that similarly with no other information? Maybe Horn just wanted to mess with me and fellow collectors. Then the 12" mixes. The first 12" release featured the "Twelve Wild Disciples Mix" on the A, the second featured the "Turn of the Knife Mix", and the third the "Attack Mix". All three featured three B-sides, the "Instrumental", "Return" and "End" mixes. Then came the new CD release, replacing the cassette singles of old- oh, what's that? Sorry, they did BOTH?!!

Yes, so Horn decided to put out a cassette single and a CD single, and make them different from each other, just for maximum fuckery! However, upon breaking down the problem, I've found that they both consist of mostly recycled materials - namely the pre-existing 12" and 7" mixes - and they only actually introduce a few new versions. First is the four second "A matter of life and death…" spoken word excerpt, which appears exclusively on the cassette release. Then there's the "Introduction" mix, the full version of which appears exclusively on the CD. And finally the "Monopoly Re-Solution", which appears on both. I've put "A matter of life and death…" and "Introduction" at the start of the disc, since they open their respective releases.

And now to the final single of the album, the only one which sounds like a pop song: "Watching the Wildlife". Still, just because it breaks from the style of the album doesn't mean it's not Frankie: they tried to distribute each single with a condom! Unfortunately they couldn't because of chart rules (it wouldn't be allowed into the singles chart if they did it), but it's the thought that counts. As for the remixes, there's still plenty. The 7" mix is actually different from the album this time, so that will be included. The B-side for the 7", and closing the B-side of all 12" releases, was "The Waves". As for those 12"s, the first featured the "Hotter" mix, as well as two unlisted tracks and the "Voiceless" mix. The second release featured "Movement 2" on the A and two further "Bits", following the hidden tracks on the first release. The third 12" release was a bit of an oddity: it was made to look like a German import, with German titles, catalogue numbers and real German remixers credited (which got them in trouble), when in actuality it was just another 12" release made by the record label's staff. This featured "Die Letzten Tage der Menschheit Mix", which translates as "The Last Days of Mankind Mix". The B-sides on this single were the same as the first 12" release. And, luckily for us, the cassette single featured no new material, and there wasn't a CD release this time.
