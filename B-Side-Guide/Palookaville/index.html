B-Side Guide

Fatboy Slim - Palookaville

Fatboy Slim's final studio album clearly resonates the end throughout its runtime. It is his only album, in my opinion, which doesn't sound original: the previous three were all brave new steps in a genre with very little rules, but to me this feels like trying to recreate the success of previous records. If you want to hear what I'm talking about, play "The Rockafeller Skank" from Fatboy's second album, and then "Slash Dot Dash" from here. So if Fatboy is treading familiar territory, then it would be obvious that one of his most familiar bastions of work to repeat here is the B-side. Here's what we have to pick through.

The lead single, the aforementioned "Slash Dot Dash", had two B-sides: "Close to Home" and the little golden nugget "What They're Looking For". Also featured on this single is two remixes, the "DJ Delite" versions of the A-Side and album track "Jin Go Lo Ba". The second single "Wonderful Night" featured only remixes of the song, three specifically, with no B-sides. The third single "The Joker" featured "The River Card" on the B.

After Palookaville, which sold pretty poorly (compared to his previous records), I'd imagine the writing was on the wall for Fatboy. Big beat as a genre was sinking rapidly as people grew tired of the genre, especially as it became ever more commercialised. So, in 2006, the standard "my career is over" trophy was released: the greatest hits album. The album featured two new songs, "That Old Pair of Jeans" and "Champion Sound", which were both released as singles. My general rule with these kinds of songs is to put them onto the bonus disc of the nearest album, and so we will do that here. The only other song worth mentioning from these two is the self-remix of "Champion Sound" included on the single. And, as a bonus, I'll add Fatboy's cover of Kraftwerk's "Radioactivity" which was released in 2007, and included on the You've Come a Long Way, Baby tenth anniversary bonus disc for no apparent reason.

Okay, they were quite thorough with their collection. However, there do seem to be some other anomalies on their disc, namely the first three tracks. "Cowboy", "Radioactivity" and "Because We Can". The first was made for the Blade II film, which was released in 2002? Seems a bit off the mark for a bonus track for an album from 1998, especially since he did an album in 2000. Even worse, the second, "Radioactivity" (a rather bonkers cover of the Kraftwerk classic), appeared on Fatboy's Late Night Tales mix album in… 2007?! Considering this was just a year before the tenth anniversary disc's release, this was probably included to be like "look, new music! ooooo look at this". Lastly, "Because We Can" was for the Moulin Rouge! film, released in 2001, one year after Fatboy Slim's album… after this one. Again, it's a bit far to reach in order to count it as a bonus track. Our disc won't include any of these then. However, the one other non-B-side on this disc, "Lounge Island", is allowed to stay, as it is a Fatboy remix of "Love Island", a track from this album. Speaking of which, the "Manumission Mix" of that track also counts, as it was also made by Fatboy himself in 1999. So, since that's rather a lot to add or remove, let's lay this out.
