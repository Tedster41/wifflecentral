Underworld - Second Toughest in the Infants

Underworld's second electronic album, and my personal favourite, is different from their first not just in stylistic terms, but also because it didn't have a bunch of singles surrounding it. The band only released one real single from this album, "Pearl's Girl". Great! So only a couple B-sides then, right? …yeah, no. Since there was only one single, they completely overcompensated and released the single with eight B-sides. EIGHT. How did this happen? Well, the first problem is that this single was released twice. You see, in February 1996, the film Trainspotting was released. Featured on it, among other amazing songs, was Underworld's "Dark & Long" (Dark Train) and "Born Slippy .NUXX". While "Dark & Long" we have previously discussed, "Born Slippy .NUXX" was released as a non-album single in 1995. The song was used over the ending of the film, and became quite well known as a result. Underworld began to gain attention, but the "Born Slippy" single had been out of print for a year, and instead they issued their new single, "Pearl's Girl", in May. Under pressure from DJs and clubbers alike, in June they finally re-released "Born Slippy .NUXX" as a single, causing their popularity to explode as the song debuted at number 2 in the UK singles chart. And so, with this newfound popularity under their belt, they re-released "Pearl's Girl" in October for their new fans, with new B-sides.

The tracklist below is roughly the same as the US "Pearl's Girl" October single release, the only differences being that the original and edited versions of "Pearl's Girl" have been replaced with "Pearl's Girl" (Carp Dreams…Koi) and "Rowla" (Extended) - this is because "Pearl's Girl" is on the album and we don't do edits here, plus neither new track were featured on the original disc. The former is a version of "Pearl's Girl" and the latter is… complicated. The intros of "Rowla" and B-side "Cherry Pie" are exactly the same, and the song itself resembles "Pearl's Girl" somewhat. And since there is a special extended version of the track only available on promotional releases, I felt I might as well put it on the "Pearl's Girl" disc.

So, yeah. That's quite a few. But what about "Born Slippy"? That wasn't on the album, as I mentioned, so what do we do with it? Well, it was included on a bonus disc in re-releases of Second Toughest, again for their new fans. On later re-releases (like the 20th anniversary one), the song has become associated with this album. So, it's best we assemble the "Born Slippy" single here. Like "Dark Train" from the dubnobass set, the version of ".NUXX" here is shorter than usual because it is segued with ".TELEMATIC", as it was presented on the original CD single.
