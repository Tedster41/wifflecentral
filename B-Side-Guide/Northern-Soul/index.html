M People - Northern Soul

If you've been in the CD section of a charity shop in the UK, then you've probably heard of M People. A project of Haçenda's Mike Pickering (who's first name gives us the 'M' in M People), M People became well known for their hits "Moving On Up" and "Search for the Hero", among a multitude of other singles. However, before their breakthrough, they released their debut album Northern Soul. Originally released in early 1992, the album boasted 10 original songs and one cover of "Someday" by CeCe Rogers. Then, when the band had a couple new songs and no album to put them on, they re-released it with a changed tracklist later that year. Their new songs were added, and some were dropped. And while there are no original B-sides for this album (they're all remixes), when there's two versions of an album with varying tracklists, that's just as good!

Now, the standardised version of the album is the 1992 re-release, mainly because that was more popular as they had a little more chart success at that point. If you want to listen to that, fine. But the version below is a combination of both versions, with all of the original tracks put together onto one album. In areas where the position of a song changes between releases (e.g. track 4 in the first release and track 7 in the second), I've either chosen one of the two spots given for the track to go into, or just found somewhere in the middle where it fits.

Note: If the original release is not noted, it means the song was present in the same form on both releases.

As previously mentioned, there are no B-sides, only remixes. However, Mike Pickering, being an established house producer, made a good few remixes himself. So, let's have a quick review of M People's first singles. Their debut was "Colour My Life" in 1991, led by the Oakenfold/Osbourne version. Following this was "How Can I Love You More?" later that same year. Then the confusion begins, with a re-release of "Colour My Life", this time led by "Part One", a mix done by M People. Then followed "Someday", led again by the "Part One" version, although the Sasha remix of the track would appear on promo releases and later the re-released version of the album. Then the lead single from the re-release, "Excited", which was released in September 1992. And finally, one last re-release for the road, a Sasha remix of "How Can I Love You More?" was released in January 1993.

Alright, that's fine, but there's one other part of the story worth mentioning. As I said earlier, upon release of the single "Someday" in 1992, some remixes were made for promotional 12” by a DJ called Sasha. Now, Sasha was not very well known at the time, but he had been doing stuff in the industry. However, after a year or two, in 1994, he and his friend John Digweed would release Renaissance: The Mix Collection, which both then and now has been called the greatest DJ mix ever put to disc. Alright, good start. He'd go further, releasing some singles in 1996, releasing one of the greatest trance EPs ever in 1999, and finally a proper album (not his first, but everyone forgot about his first) in 2002. But the first time he'd get somewhere in the charts was in 1993, when he did another remix for M People, this time on "How Can I Love You More?", off of this album. Yes, after both versions of the album had been put out, the band had one more go on the re-release horse and best of all, this single reached no. 8 in the UK. So Sasha seemed to be rather good at helping out the M People. Does this mean I have to make a disc of Sasha remixes?

…sure, why not.

As a quick note before we move on, "Sasha's Qat Mix" is listed there as being released in 1998. Despite this, it was recorded at the same time as the '93 remixes, but went unreleased… kind of. Remember that DJ mix CD I mentioned earlier, the one that has been called the greatest one of its kind? Well, the "Qat Mix" is played on that. Not in its full length, or course not, but a portion of it. So while it was released in its entirety in 1998, the clubbing public has had a taste of it since September 1994.
