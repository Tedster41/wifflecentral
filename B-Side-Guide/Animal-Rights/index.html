Moby - Animal Rights

Moby is one of those artists that you hear about and don't realise how many songs of his you know. The first half of his fourth album, Play, should be largely familiar to the average person on the street. With its sampled hooks and mainly chilled-out beats, it came to define the music featured in advertising in the early 2000s. Play's B-sides have been collected on several occasions, so instead I am collecting the B-sides from the previous album, Animal Rights, which is the most out-of-the-ordinary album in Moby's whole career. Rather than the electronic productions of his entire discography, Animal Rights is Moby's brief moment where he expresses his teenage self, not through the synthesiser or drum machine, but through the guitar - and then fed through a distortion pedal. Naturally, the album strongly alienated his entire fanbase and is critically regarded as the worst of his career, but personally I think it is thoroughly underrated, with tracks like "Come On Baby" having more raw energy than any of his techno tunes before it, and Moby's skills on show in the solos, particularly in "Living", are worth some applause at least.

Before we get to the B-sides, it's worth noting that Moby didn't abandon everything he had built up in his career at that point; the album still, like the one before this (Everything is Wrong), featured a bonus disc of ambient tracks with a limited edition version. Titled Little Idiot, this is much more familiar territory for the man, but still features some reworking of tracks on the main album.

Now onto the main job. Despite Moby being an American, the UK was his biggest market (since techno was big there), and so the album was released in the UK a while before it was in the States. Upon its release in the States, the tracklist was re-ordered and added to, some tracks being taken from Little Idiot, but two are exclusive to this edition. The Japanese edition, naturally, featured a bonus track also: a cover of Joy Division's "New Dawn Fades". And as for the B-sides: the lead single "That's When I Reach for My Revolver" featured a chunky five B-sides, while second single "Come On Baby" featured two.

Some keen-eyed Moby fans will notice that I have not included Moby's version of the James Bond theme, which was released as a single in 1997, even though this is the nearest album to it. The sole reason is that the song is a techno track, and simply does not fit with the whole style of this album, so it'll be left off.
