BT - Movement in Still Life

With his third album, Movement in Still Life, BT completed his transition from his prior state of making merely mortal trance tunes to making works of which the descriptions lie beyond the realm of language. Once again, the huge amount of effort required to make the album is clear from the beginning; the level of detail is exquisite, beyond that of most electronic music albums. However, while BT had changed a lot since his debut, one thing remained consistent: this album is a massive mess. Again, like his previous albums, not a mess in the sense that it's a bad album, not at all, but rather the tracklist is all over the place.

To start with the slightly easier stuff: the main version of the album has the tracks mixed into one another, but some versions include unmixed versions of the tracks on the album. By compiling these special versions of the albums and a few singles, you can actually manage to get an entirely unmixed version of the album, with all the same songs. This will be our first bonus disc. The one issue with this, however, is that unmixed versions are longer than mixed versions, and so not all the songs fit. Therefore, two songs will have to be moved to another disc - I'll explain my reasoning for the songs I chose to exclude ("Ride" and "Namistai") when we get to them.

Now, onto the chaos of the bonus tracks. First is "Fibonacci Sequence". This track is pretty much part of the album, because it appears on every special edition or bonus disc that it was ever released with, so I'd imagine BT might have wanted to put it on the record but there wasn't enough space. After this, we have a bunch of tracks which only appear on certain editions of the album. "Sunblind" was a bonus track on the Japanese edition, "Never Gonna Come Back Down", "Shame", "Smartbomb" and "Love on Haight Street" were all exclusively released on the 2000 North American edition, and "Far From Goodbye" appeared on a UK promotional sampler of the album, and then wasn't released anywhere else. Then there's a few remixes to put on the end, all done by BT of course: a special remix of "Madskillz-Mic Chekka" for the movie Zoolander (yes really) in 2001, a Dub Mix of "Mercury and Solace", the Libra remix of "Dreaming" (Libra being an alias of BT) and a remix of "Smartbomb".

But we're not done yet! We haven't brought up those two tracks I cut earlier, nor have we mentioned a spare bit involving BT's collaborations with other artists. Firstly, if you've read the other BT articles (in particular Ima), you'll know he has an affinity with a DJ called Sasha. The two of them collaborated a number of times, and one of their last pieces of work together was The Real World Session - a rare CD consisting of three songs that the pair made in 1998 at Peter Gabriel's Real World Studios. Gabriel had apparently brought the two together again to work on some music for him, but a number of issues (including BT's studio being robbed twice) meant the plans never came to fruition. But, we still have those three songs… and in fact, they made one other song together in 1998: "Ride". Yes, the song from earlier that I dropped from the unmixed album tracklist was a collaboration between BT and Sasha, originally released as a single-sided 12" under the name "2 Phat Cunts". So this will join the tracks from The Real World Session. But this isn't the only collaboration to be featured on this disc, as the other track we dropped from the unmixed disc, "Namistai", was a collaboration with Paul van Dyk, with whom BT had made "Flaming June", the lead single from his previous album, ESCM. "Namistai" would also appear on van Dyk's album Out There and Back, which I felt justified taking it off the unmixed disc since it felt a bit more 'disconnected' from the album. A dual-citizen, if you will. And while I did debate putting a couple remixes by the trance group Hybrid on the end of this disc (because album track "Running Down the Way Up" was co-produced with Hybrid), I decided against it as the argument that they aren't external remixes was a little too weak.
