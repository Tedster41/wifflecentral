The Beatles - 1969–Onwards (The Solo Years)

If you're a Beatles fan, and even if you're not one, you should be familiar with the Red and Blue albums. These two double-disc sets compiled what the Fab Four had to offer over their eight years recording music together. And even though they recorded much more music than most artists in those eight years, Beatles fans will always be thirsty for more. Therefore, salvation comes in the form of the solo career: all four Beatles put out tons of stuff on their own, some making even more than what they did in the Beatle days. Naturally, some have attempted to make additional Red and Blue albums for these solo periods, the most famous of these being The Black Album, however I have decided to take my own shot at it. Why? Well, because the most popular option does not include "Nineteen Hundred and Eighty Five", nor "Monkberry Moon Delight", so I must put in a protest vote against this terrible regime. JUSTICE FOR SOUP AND PUREE!

ahem - So, here is my little collection, divided into three parts of two CDs each. The Green Album (1969–1973 - it's 1969 rather than 1970 because of "Give Peace a Chance" and "Cold Turkey"), the Yellow Album (1974–1980) and the Orange Album (1981–2026). Yes, the Orange album covers a far longer period when compared to the rest, but the 70s were big for ex-Beatles, while the 80s and beyond weren't really. Each album is compiled in the style of the original Red and Blue albums, which put focus on the singles, and less on 'deep cuts' on the albums. Yes, the 2023 versions have since corrected for that and added more nuance, but given we've gone from one powerhouse to four, some things have to be cut. And, like the Red and Blue albums, everything is ordered chronologically, although there is the occasional slight shift for the sake of flow from track to track. Because there's so much stuff to fill in, there's differences between the CD versions and the vinyl versions (yes I've come up with vinyl versions). For the Green Album, since it has four records, some songs have to be cut for the CD. But for the Yellow and Orange albums, since they're only three records each, some tracks are added for the CD. These tracks will appear in grey.
