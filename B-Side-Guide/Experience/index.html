The Prodigy - Experience

The Prodigy's Experience is certainly quite an experience of a record. Riding high on the breakbeat sound of '92, this is the Prodigy at their earliest and really the only time when they sounded like the rest of the scene, when breakbeat raves in warehouses were everywhere in the UK. Much later down the line, the album was re-released with an 'Expanded' disc, consisting of the tracks on the singles surrounding the album. I have to admit, the compilers did a good job in putting it together, but they didn't cover everything. So here's what they missed:

Before I get into a run-down of the band's discography, I should just mention that the Prodigy isn't a typical band in the sense that only one person, Liam Howlett, actually makes the music. Maxim does vocals live (as heard on the last track of the album), while Keith Flint and Leeroy Thornhill were dancers. The former two would later begin to contribute properly to the band's albums in 1996, with Flint doing vocals on "Firestarter" and Maxim on "Breathe", both tracks being no. 1 hits in the UK.

Now onto the discography side of things. First, the band was signed to XL Recordings in late 1990, and they put out their first EP, What Evil Lurks, in February 1991. The EP consisted of songs on Howlett's demo tapes, which we have already covered elsewhere. Following this was "Charly", the band's first single, and already they were onto a winner. The track, which samples a public service announcement film for children, reached number 3 on the UK charts, and its B-side "Your Love" became a very popular track amongst DJs to bring the night to a close. They followed this up with "Everybody in the Place", which reached higher to number 2. Both tracks would appear in remixed form on the album, and would have three B-sides each.

Then, the album was released in September 1992, accompanied by the single "Fire" / "Jericho". Following this was by far the most popular single from the album, "Out of Space", which is still well known today. And then finally, "Wind It Up (Rewound)", a remix of album track "Wind It Up", was released in 1993, by which point Howlett declared he was fed up with releasing songs from the same album and wanted to move onto the next one. Put all of these together and… we run into a slight problem. We have to ditch one track for it to all fit on a CD. I've elected to remove what I think to be the least significant track: the original version of "Everybody in the Place", the last track on the What Evil Lurks EP. You can obviously ditch whatever track you like (especially as the track I removed is the shortest on the album, so any other option will make it fit), or even try burning a disc with over 80 minutes on it (scary)! Either way, here's the (almost entirely) assembled singles collection from this era of the Prodigy:
