B-Side Guide

Faithless - Reverence

If you've heard the name Faithless, then chances are you've heard their greatest hit, "Insomnia". That 90s track that goes "I can't get no sleep", if you're British then chances are you know it. Well, Reverence, released in early 1996, was the album from which that hit spawned. This album doesn't have any original B-side tracks. However, it has an absurd amount of remixes made by its creators. Look, let me show you.

The first single from this album, "Salva Mea", was released in 1995. The original 12” featured the Epic Mix on the A-side, and the Tuff Mix on the B. However the double disc promo release, and many international releases, featured the Sister Bliss Remix and Floating Mix in addition. The album version of the song is a slight tweak of the Epic Mix. When "Insomnia" got popular the following year, this song was re-released with some new remixes: The '96 Remix and Slow Version which appeared on CD releases, while the Deep Gold Mix appeared on the occasional 'remixes' single. Putting all of the remixes together, you get this.

Alright, and then after "Salva Mea" was released, the group released their second single, "Insomnia". Upon original release, it also had four remixes, two on the main single, and two on the promo. The two on the main single, the Monster Mix and Moody Mix, would be combined to make the album version. Most remixes after this were done externally, but the group did make a '96 Remix for its (highly anticipated) re-release following a campaign by DJs such as Pete Tong. The song was also re-released in 1997 for the American market, with remixes by Armand van Helden, with one of those mixes (the European Vacation Mix) becoming one of the most popular versions of the song.

Alright, so the two trance/prog house tracks from the album have been released, which just leaves us with some rap numbers from Maxi Jazz and Jamie Catto's other projects. Alright, so no more B-side remixes, right? Nope. We've still got a whole lot more to cover.

After the first release of "Insomnia", the third single was released, the Catto ballad "Don't Leave". So, how did the producers of the band, Rollo & Sister Bliss, react upon having to release a ballad as a single? Did Catto write a few other B-side numbers? Of course not. More remixes!

This single is slightly different to the previous ones, as the original release didn't have four remixes, but six! Three remixes appeared on the UK release, and three on the release in Mainland Europe. The European remixes would be released in the UK in 1997 upon the re-release of the single, along with two new (much more faithful to the original) versions.

You may have noticed that once again, there was a second release of this single. That was not because of the success of "Insomnia", but rather because it was featured in the film A Life Less Ordinary. The version featured there is "Goetz's String Mix", Goetz being Faithless' regular engineer. The mix itself is simply just the original but now with a string section, so I felt it was alright to include. The "Orchestral String Mix" is different, and was released only on a US promo CD. The remix is credited to Rollo & Sister Bliss, so again it's alright to include.

So, that's all of them, right? Still no.

After "Don't Leave" was "If Lovin' You is Wrong", one of Maxi Jazz's rap numbers, which was released as a single in July 1996 after the original release of "Don't Leave". And guess what it had? Remixes!

Okay, surely that's- In 1997, the group released another single from the album, the title track, "Reverence". Once again, it wasn't really suited to be remixed (it's another rap number), but it got remixes anyway. However, it seems by this point Rollo & Sister Bliss were really running low on steam, because only two remixes were created for it. This is so little we can tack it onto our previous "Don't Leave" disc.
