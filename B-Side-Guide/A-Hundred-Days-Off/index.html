B-Side Guide

Underworld - A Hundred Days Off

The B-side boys are back once again, now without Darren Emerson, who launched the 'Mk II' era of electronic music under the Underworld name. So, can the boys still pull it off? Of course they can! Emerson wasn't even given writing credits on most of their tracks before this, so what's stopping them from continuing? Considering they're still active today, I'd imagine not much. Still, this album was a turn from their usual output, spending more time hanging around in ambient places, but bringing the energy with the two singles "Two Months Off" and "Dinosaur Adventure 3D".

Okay, so what have the group famous for their excessive B-sides (on this page at least) got for us now? Uh... four B-sides. Not much, but still worth something. The album had two singles, the aforementioned "Two Months Off" and "Dinosaur Adventure 3D", both of which had two B-sides each. "Headset" and "Tiny Clicks" for the former, "Ansum" and "Like a Swimmer" for the latter. Oh, and a "R.C.M. Version" of "Dinosaur Adventure 3D" made by the group. So, five tracks. Great. Is there anything more? Not on the B-side plane, no. However, Underworld, as previously mentioned, are known for their copious amount of studio outtakes and rare tidbits. And does this have any? Yes.

As documented on the Wikipedia page for the album, a fan released a rare promo version of the album online in Spring 2011. This promo version, titled "Ansum", has an altered tracklist, and while most songs are the exact same, some appear in different forms. To quote the page, ""Mo Move" is doubled in length and shares its second half with a reworked version of the B-side "Ansum"; "Twist" contains an extended drum outro; and "Ballet Lane" and "Trim" contain minor percussion differences." Discogs also notes that "Two Months Off" has noticable differences in mixing, particularly in percussion and reverb. Alright, so let's add all of those on the end of our bonus disc. I couldn't find any other rarities, but this is more than enough.
