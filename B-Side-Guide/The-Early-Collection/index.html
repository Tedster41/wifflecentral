Sasha - The Early Collection

Legendary DJ Sasha is most well known for his work as a DJ in the 90s, when he mixed compilations with his friend John Digweed, such as Renaissance: The Mix Collection and Northern Exposure - both now regarded as some of the best mix CDs ever made (good luck finding them for cheap!). He also began 'producing' music in the early 90s, with his most famous work being that made in the decade. I say 'producing' because it was well known that he had people working with him who, allegedly, did most of the work. Whether he still does this I'm not sure, but this was definitely the case in the 90s. However, despite being his most successful period as an artist, only one album was issued throughout the whole decade, and that album consisted of probably the worst of the stuff he put out in the era. So, this aims to recreate what a box set collecting Sasha's 90s works would look like.

Sasha started with Tom Frederikse by his side, producing mainly remixes, but in 1994 the two made The Qat Collection, a full length album which Sasha has largely erased from his history, as a result of its dated sound, long instrumentals, and poor reception from critics. Personally, I think it's a great album, demonstrating Frederikse's unique take on Euro House in the mid-90s. The album was first released in 1994, and featured six tracks.

From here, there were no more albums: we're on our own. Possibly due to the negative reception of The Qat Collection, Sasha ditched Frederikse and hired Richard Dekkard to help out, with whom Sasha's next single, "Be as One", was produced. Released in 1996, the single is one of Sasha's most well known, and features vocals from Maria Nayler, who would later collaborate with Robert Miles and Tilt. It's backed by "Heart of Imagination", which was made with help from Sasha's good friend BT, who I have covered at length elsewhere.

Following "Be as One" was another single in 1996, "Arkham Asylum", backed with "Ohmna". Dekkard later stated that this release was actually largely made by Sasha, with him only helping out for a little synth programming. Dekkard had been hired to effectively teach Sasha how to produce so that he could work on his first 'proper' album, which would turn out as Airdrawndagger, released in 2002. Dekkard felt that by "Arkham Asylum", Sasha had learnt how to do so, and left him to it. Sasha would do another track the following year, "The Other Side", which he produced as the B-side for Maria Nayler's single "Naked and Scared". It was released under Nayler's name, but since it's produced by him, matches the style of the rest of the tracks (it's an eleven-minute long prog trance track similar to "Arkham Asylum"), and is all the Discogs commenters rant about on that release, it'll be put on our disc too. It's unclear if Dekkard helped with this track.

By the way, the title of this disc comes from Sasha (and Digweed's) 1996 mix CD Northern Exposure.

In 1999, Sasha made a return with a new friend: Charlie May. May has become a consistent member of Sasha's entourage, helping in the production of Airdrawndagger and other Sasha projects. Their first project together was Sasha's most famous work, the Xpander EP. Featuring four tracks, it's longer than a number of LPs, because each track is over ten minutes, but because there's only four tracks, an EP it is. But Xpander wasn't the only thing Sasha did in 1999! The developers of the third game in the Wipeout franchise, aptly titled Wipeout 3, hired Sasha to compile the soundtrack for the game. Given it's is a futuristic racer where you drive a spaceship at 250mph, the most up-to-date electronic music seemed to be a good choice. Sasha included five short (by his standards) original tracks, each named after a team in the game, as well as an edit of his previously released "Xpander". It's unclear if May helped with these tracks. Also included on the soundtrack were songs by Underworld, Orbital, Paul van Dyk and Propellerheads. And finally, in 2000, Sasha released "Scorchio", a collaboration with former Underworld member Darren Emerson (as well as May of course), which became popular in the clubs and on trance compilation albums. The single cover was in a similar style to that of "Xpander", so I'm including it here.

Personally, the second disc, featuring five long progressive trance tracks which wind and float their way through the hour, is my personal favourite. I'd favour the latter two discs over Airdrawndagger, which is still a pretty good release.
