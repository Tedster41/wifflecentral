B-Side Guide

System F - Out of the Blue

After numerous attempts over many, many aliases from 1990 through to 1998, a young Ferry Corsten finally had a hit. "Out of the Blue", which he produced in 1998, was released the following year to much attention in the dance world. Soon after, Corsten was invited to mix Ministry of Sound's new Trance Nation compilation series, and he would go on to win the 'Zilveren Harp', a Dutch award for promising talent, later in 1999. Now finally with a successful moniker, Corsten took to making an album, which he originally intended to be released in October 1999. However, the album was delayed until 2000 for unknown reasons. And so, late that year, when trance was at an all-time high, his debut album as System F, Out of the Blue, was finally released… in Japan. It was another four months before everyone else got a hold on it. But the point was, the album was out! And as often happens with electronic music albums, a lot of the big hits, including the title track, are present only in radio edit form. Now, I always prefer full-length versions over radio edits, so before we get into B-sides, let's start by replacing the edits with their full-length originals. Only problem is, if we do them all, it won't fit within the eighty-minute limit of a CD. Okay, what now? Well, in a bygone era, long before CDs were invented, music enjoyers like you and I listened to music through different discs, known as vinyl records. These mystical discs could store music not in digital form, but in analogue. Without messing with the volume or fidelity of the record, it can store (roughly) up to 22 minutes per side, which is probably the reason why we moved to CDs. However, since these discs, unlike CDs, have two sides, it means as much as 44 minutes of music can be safely stored on a record. Still not great. However, if we multiply and gain another record, we can store 88 minutes of music, 8 minutes more than a single CD. And sure enough, this is enough to fit all the full-length versions.

What's strange is the original album did get a vinyl release, but with the radio edits included instead of the full length versions, despite the fact that they evidently could include them. Either way, you have the concept for it now.

Now with the main album sorted, it's on to the B-sides! Let's start with the title track. On its original release on Corsten's own Tsunami label in 1999, the track came with a "Super Secret" remix on the B-side, which Corsten had made. I'm not too sure what's so secret about it; it's a regular remix. Later when the song found success, Corsten made a new remix of the song, the "5AM Mix". After "Out of the Blue", Corsten had a big follow-up in 2000 with "Cry". The original release was accompanied by the "Ferry Corsten Clubmix" on the B-side, while the main commercial release had the "DotNL Mix", which is basically an attempt to make "Cry" sound as much like "Out of the Blue" as possible. I'm not joking, listen to it. After this we have a few singles with no B-sides, but a couple bonus originals. Firstly upon the album's early release in Japan, it came accompanied with a bonus track (as if getting the album four months early wasn't good enough), "The Game". Later in 2001, "Dance Valley Theme 2001" would be released as a stand-alone single. This song has generally been claimed for this album, as it was released only a few months after the album, featured album track "Mode Confusion" on the B-side, and it appeared on the 2010 bonus disc alongside the remastered version of this album. We'll get to that bonus disc in a minute. "Solstice", the last main single of the album (and a vinyl-only one at that), had a "Second Edition" of "Out of the Blue" which brought the then four-year old production up to where trance was at that moment. Then, there is one more single released from this album, far-off in 2007. As Corsten had founded a new record label, Flashover Recordings, in 2005, Tsunami was going… underused. Corsten's partner who he founded Tsunami with wasn't releasing music anymore, and so the label would have a few more releases in 2005 and 2006, almost all Corsten's material from his last Tsunami album, Right of Way, before the label died in 2007. But Corsten rightfully felt that the label should have its swan song rather than dying simply of obsolescence, and so he came back to this album and picked out "Insolation" to remix into a ten-minute epic as the A-side to Tsunami's final single and the only release on the label in 2007. "Insolation" also has an "Original Extended" version, the origins of which I cannot trace, which has been available digitally since 2007. Lastly, that bonus disc I mentioned earlier. In 2010, Out of the Blue was re-released with a bonus disc of remixes and additional stuff, some of which we've covered here. Most of the disc is external remixes so are of no bother to us, but the one addition which I will mention is the "Violin Edit" of "Out of the Blue" present on the disc. It was made in 2007 for Corsten's Full on Ferry show, and had been used as a DJ tool ever since. This version is roughly the same as the original, but features a new breakdown with a string section playing the melody. One other track I will mention on this disc is the Armin van Buuren remix of "Exhale", which considering Buuren co-wrote the original, would count, but doesn't fit on our disc. We'll have to leave it off here, but keep it in mind otherwise.
