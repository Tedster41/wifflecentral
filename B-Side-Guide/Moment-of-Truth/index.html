Man With No Name - Moment of Truth

(The?) Man With No Name is a pseudonym of trance artist Martin Freeland who specialises in 'Goa' trance, a form of the genre popularised by Paul Oakenfold which focuses on moody melodic works with more focus on psychedelic mid-90s druggy madness than the "put your hands up" sort of stuff. Freeland, especially under his Man With No Name alias, is one of the most revered artists of the genre, and I would strongly recommend starting with this album (and the single "Teleport"!), as well as Oakenfold's seminal "Goa Mix", if you want to get a feel for the genre.

Now, as for the additional tracks, we best start with Freeland's most interesting choice: making songs for compilation albums. There's plenty of Goa trance compilation albums, the sorts with the 90s computer graphics multi-coloured covers with patterns all over and names like "Psychic Dancefloor". Before he had made a name for himself with his singles (and this album), Freeland made songs exclusively for these compilations. The two big ones we're interested in are Digital Alchemy and Tribal Science (see what I mean about those names?). For the former, he made five exclusives, although two would appear on Moment of Truth, one in a remixed form and the other as it was on the original compilation. For the latter, he made two exclusives. As well as this, Freeland would make one exclusive each for three further compilations: Psychedelic Techno Hard Trance Vol. 2, Earth Trance (which was actually a trance charity album), and Order Odonata (Experiments That Identify Change). Putting these all together creates an album in-and-of-itself.

The only compilation track not included is "Evolution" from 'Digital Alchemy', since as previously mentioned it is on 'Moment of Truth'. "Sugar Rush" also appears on the album, but in a "Refined Mix" form.

Now on to the regular job: singles. Firstly, I'm starting from 1993, since before then the Man With No Name alias was used for techno releases which should be their own thing rather than being presented alongside this Goa trance album. The first Goa trance single Freeland did was "Paint a Picture", with "The Void" as the B-side. The next single, in 1994, was (effectively a double A-side) "Teleport" / "Sly-Ed". Then in 1995, the first single from the album and the first single on Perfecto Records (Paul Oakenfold's record label), "Floor-Essence". While the "Dayglo Mix" is on the album, there are two additional remixes by Freeland on the single here. Also in 1995 was "Lunar Cycle", backed with "Neuro Tunnel". Then finally we have some re-releases with remixes, those of "Paint a Picture" backed with "Floor-Essence" and "Teleport" backed with "Sugar Rush". Altogether, you get this:
