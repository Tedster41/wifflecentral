B-Side Guide

Pet Shop Boys - Bilingual

The road continues for the Pet Shop Boys in what I fear might be the last of our great B-side adventures with the Boys. But we're certainly not going out with a bang - this set promises to be as complicated as ever!

We start first with a non-album single, "Paninaro '95". A remix of their 1986 B-side "Paninaro", this single was released in promotion of their B-sides compilation Alternative. This single featured two B-sides, "In the Night '95" (a remix of a 1985 B-side) and "Girls and Boys". "Girls and Boys" was included on our set for Very (see the explanation there for why), but the other B-side can stay. Now we come to the four singles from the album: "Before", "Se a vida é (That's the Way Life Is)", "Single-Bilingual" and "A Red Letter Day", which each featured two original B-sides. But of course with the Boys, it's a little more complicated than that. "Single-Bilingual" featured a remix of album track "Discoteca", which was originally intended as a single instead of "Single", but never really worked as an A-side (personally though I think it's the better of the two). "A Red Letter Day" was remixed for its release by Motiv8, who were big in the Europop scene at this time ("Ooh Aah… Just a Little Bit" by Gina G is one of my personal favourite productions of theirs). And now we continue into the world of non-album niceties: in 1997 the duo continued their series of destroying and rebuilding a classic song by doing a cover of the West Side Story showtune "Somewhere" with a techno beat and bass. The single, which was accompanied by two B-sides, was released to promote both their residency at the Savoy Theatre that summer, and a re-released special edition of Bilingual, which featured a bonus disc of remixes, as well as the extended mix of "Somewhere". Then, to push the boat out just a little further, the fan club-exclusive Christmas song: "It Doesn't Often Snow at Christmas" was released in December 1997. And finally, two alternate versions: the Moscow Mix of "A Red Letter Day" and the Orchestral Version of "Somewhere".

And now, once again it's time for the 12" mixes of your favourite album tracks, although this time we don't have to include any done by remixers who weren't involved in the original singles themselves. First, the 12" version of "Paninaro '95", done by the Pet Shop Boys. Then we include both the extended mix of "Before", made by the track's co-producer Danny Tenaglia, and a dub mix he did (I've neglected to include the "Bonus Beats" version since they're generally used for mixing and aren't of much interest, and plus it doesn't fit on the CD). Then the remix of "Discoteca" by the Pet Shop Boys which was originally intended for a single release (which is different from the "new version" featured on CD 2), which was released after the fact on the album's Further Listening set in 2001. Released with the original single in '96 was the extended mix of the "new version". Then, also from the Further Listening set, the "Expanded Single Version" of "A Red Letter Day", and from its original release, the "Motiv8 Twelve Inch Master Mix" of the song, Motiv8 being the team that produced the single version. Then comes four remixes of its B-side "The Boy Who Couldn't Keep His Clothes On", which was originally co-produced by Danny Tenaglia. Three of the remixes were featured on a promotional release sent to DJs, while one was featured as a B-side to "A Red Letter Day". Once again, I have neglected to include the "Bonus Beats" version for the same reasons as last time. And finally, the Extended Mix of "Somewhere", which strangely enough I don't think was ever released as part of the single package, and was instead exclusively included on the Special Edition of Bilingual.
