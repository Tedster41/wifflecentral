The Beatles - The Beatles CD Package

Have you ever wanted all of the Beatles' works on as few CDs as possible? Are you burning their entire discography to CD for a friend to listen to? Have you got too many CDs and a thirst for more? Do you own all of the Beatles' works already and want to do something with them? Do you ever think Past Masters feels like a totally forced tacked-on afterthought part of their official discography and in no way counts as an album? Are you far too deep into a tracklist-themed insanity spiral? If any of the above, take a look at this concept of mine: The Beatles CD Package. Taking advantage of the short duration of the Beatles' LPs (by today's standards at least), this set packs two Beatles albums together, plus additional tracks from Past Masters where required, all onto one disc. This means that despite releasing 13* studio albums (*including Magical Mystery Tour), you only need nine discs to fit all of the albums, including Past Masters (mostly - we'll get to that). This is a compacting from 16 discs to 9, almost halving the total count, so hopefully it can fit in your glovebox now. There is also a bonus disc, because every good Beatles box set needs a rarities album, but also because tracks like "My Bonnie" and "Free as a Bird" need to go somewhere if you really want to call it a full collection of everything they ever released (when the band were still around - I'm not touching stuff like Live at the Hollywood Bowl).

Now, allow me to discuss the liberties I took in making this that the Beatle-heads who need to get a life (myself included) will point out. The single version of "Love Me Do" is not included on the first disc. This is primarily because it won't fit on the disc, but one could also argue that it should be placed on the "Rarities" disc anyway since "Love Me Do" is already on the disc. The two German tracks (you know the ones) are not on CD 1 or 2 for similar reasons. CD 3 neglects to include "Bad Boy" from the American Beatles VI release; this is again because there is no room for it. CD 5 contains Magical Mystery Tour and Yellow Submarine, despite having the White Album released between them, because the two go well together and most of the original Yellow Submarine tracks were recorded in 1967, around the time Magical Mystery Tour was being recorded. "All You Need is Love" is omitted from Yellow Submarine because the track directly precedes the Yellow Submarine section of CD 5 because of its inclusion at the end of the Magical Mystery Tour section. The Beatles has bonus tracks on both discs, which while you might argue is a little unorthodox, may I point your attention to the 2001 release of All Things Must Pass which puts the bonus tracks on disc one so it can fit the Apple Jam on disc two. Plus it looks a bit more symmetrical, even vertically. If you want to put all the bonus tracks on the second disc, again that's up to you; it's your CD-Rs and it'll fit just fine. "Get Back" and "Don't Let Me Down" are on the Let It Be disc rather than the Abbey Road one for obvious reasons (despite their release date), although it does mean that the two versions of "Get Back" are right next to one another.

Quite a few there, isn't it? Well, we're not finished yet, because there's some bits and bobs to sort out, which is why we have our final disc, the special fabricated third version of Rarities!

Firstly, I just want to clarify that this isn't going to be an Anthology Highlights disc; this is all stuff that was released back in the 60s. This version of Rarities is split into four sections. The first is a collection of the Beatles' recordings made in Hamburg: "My Bonnie", "Ain't She Sweet", "Cry for a Shadow", that lot. Basically all the tracks on The Beatles' First (a.k.a. Very Together or In the Beginning) appear here, obviously excluding the four tracks they don't actually play on which snuck onto the compilation. The next three tracks are the Past Masters leftovers, those being the two German tracks and "Bad Boy". In fairness, these three were all on the original UK Rarities album. Then comes the most subjective part of this whole set: "Rare mixes". Now, there are a limited number of rare mixes, but there certainly isn't enough room on the disc for all of them, so I only included the essentials and some mono mixes which vary from the stereo versions (primarily the White Album ones) here. The obvious rare mixes you're thinking of are here: "Love Me Do" off Past Masters (which is an entirely different recording, but shut up), the German version of "And I Love Her", the US Promo version of "Penny Lane" and the Wildlife version of "Across the Universe". As for the mono mixes which vary from the original, again the obvious ones are here: "She's Leaving Home", "Helter Skelter", "Help!", "Yer Blues" and so on. And lastly, probably the ones you were thinking about this whole time in terms of "where will you put them?", "Free as a Bird", "Real Love" and "Now and Then" close the disc. I know I said no Anthology stuff but my point was "no early takes and demos released after the fact". These three aren't that, and plus they've got to go somewhere.

If you do actually want to burn this set, obviously I'd recommend you have a tinker around and switch out the mixes in particular with ones you favour - the ones here are just my personal selection based on the most popular rare mixes. So, the final (largely optional) disc of the set is the following.

Rare mixes/versions

    • Different recording than the album version with Ringo Starr on drums
  2. "And I Love Her" (German Version)
    • Edit of the standard stereo version with two additional repeats of the guitar riff on the end
  3. "Help!" (Mono Version)
    • Different vocal take used than the album version with no tambourine
  4. "I'm Only Sleeping" (UK Mono Version)
    • More backwards guitar than other versions
  5. "Tomorrow Never Knows" (Remix 11)
    • Withdrawn mono version, only on a few releases
  6. "Penny Lane" (US Promo Mix)
    • Features an additional trumpet flourish on the end
  7. "She's Leaving Home" (Mono Version)
    • Played a semitone higher than the stereo version
  8. "Glass Onion" (Mono Version)
    • More sound effects than the stereo version
  9. "Don't Pass Me By" (Mono Version)
    • Played faster and with more fiddle
  10. "Yer Blues" (Mono Version)
    • Longer outro than the stereo version
  11. "Helter Skelter" (Mono Version)
    • Shorter outro than the stereo version
  12. "Across the Universe" (Wildlife Version)
