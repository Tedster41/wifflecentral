B-Side Guide

Pet Shop Boys - Very

After releasing their darkest and most minor key album yet, Behaviour, the Pet Shop Boys made a new record as a reaction to the critical success but commercial mediocrity of the record by making the most bubblegum, europop album they could. Filled with early 90s rave sounds, M1 pianos, TR-909 drum machines and so on, this album represents a major shift in the style of the band. Leaving behind their neutral faces and largely white album covers, they made a record with music videos in bright fluorescent colours made primarily of CGI materials. I think the record is so different from the Pet Shop Boys’ previous work that it sounds like a completely different band (I'd say only "To Speak is a Sin" sounds anything at all like their previous album; naturally its one of my favourites). However, you can tell it's still them because of the plethora of B-sides! So let’s get digging.

First, it should be mentioned that upon release, Very came with a limited edition bonus disc titled Relentless. Relentless consisted of six instrumental tracks which were more experimental than the pop-styled album tracks. Obviously, this will be in our set.

Now, onto the actual B-sides. The number of B-sides per single varies. “Can You Forgive Her?” and “I Wouldn't Normally Do This Kind of Thing” had two B-sides, “Yesterday, When I Was Mad” had three, while “Go West” and “Liberation” only had one each. There are some other things to mention: “I Wouldn’t Normally Do This Kind of Thing” and “Yesterday, When I Was Mad” were remixed for the single release by the Beatmasters and Jam & Spoon respectively, so those remixes will be on our disc. Also released during this album cycle was “Absolutely Fabulous”, credited to the same name, which was a Pet Shop Boys one-off production consisting of clips from the show Absolutely Fabulous over a Very-style beat. Since PSB themselves included this on their Further Listening release of Very, I think it’s alright to include it here. Also on that release is a live version of “Girls and Boys” by Blur. PSB had been hired to produce a remix of the song in 1994, which ended up being quite successful, and so they performed it themselves on the Discovery tour which followed this album. And my one exception for my “no live recordings” rule is when the live recording is of a new song, so this counts. The only other bits to add are two remixes of these B-sides, the Unplugged Mix of B-side “Decadence” and the “Dull Soulless Dance Music Mix” of “Absolutely Fabulous” (it makes sense if you listen to it). Now, that’s quite a bit, isn’t it?

Just in case anyone was wondering, “Violence” (track 6) is a song off PSB’s debut, Please. It was remixed in 1992 for a performance at the Haçienda nightclub, hence the name. And fun fact, it was at that same performance that the Pet Shop Boys first played “Go West”, before it had even been released, as a tribute to the lives lost as a result of AIDS (which was what the concert was for). Like another one of their hits, “Always on My Mind”, they considered releasing the cover as a non-album single as the performance was so successful. And what happened to that original version? Well…

With Behaviour, we had a lot of 12” mixes to deal with. Here, rather than making the 12” mixes themselves, the Pet Shop Boys embraced the style of dance music by having someone remix their singles for the 12” mix. It's those 12” mixes which will be the primary content of this disc, but there’s some other oddities as well. To start with, in 1992 there was a very limited acetate release of “Go West”, in a version which is similar to the album version, but is nine minutes long instead of five. This was originally meant to be a test pressing of the non-album single I mentioned earlier, but the Pet Shop Boys pulled out when they realised they’d written “Can You Forgive Her?”, which would also be a banger single, so they put “Go West” on the album and had it be the second single instead. So this version of “Go West”, as well as its intended B-side “Forever in Love” (which ended up in a remixed form on Relentless) are at the start of this disc, being sourced from the Further Listening set I mentioned earlier. The A-side mix of “Can You Forgive Her?” is by Rollo, who didn’t work on the original, but it’s the 12” A-side so fine. “Go West” had an A-side remixed by Brothers in Rhythm, who assisted on the original, so that definitely counts. The Beatmasters, who were responsible for the main single remix of “I Wouldn't Normally Do This Kind of Thing” mentioned earlier, also did the 12” mix so that definitely counts. The main mix of “Liberation” was done by E Smoove, who didn’t have an involvement in the original but it stays as its the A-side on 12” releases. On remix editions of this single, it was backed with a remix of album track “Young Offender”. Since it’s done by Jam & Spoon (who I’ll get to in just a second) and there’s room for it, this version also goes on the disc. Jam & Spoon remixed “Yesterday, When I Was Mad” for the main single release and also did the 12” mix, so that’s here as well, and finally, the strangest mix of them all (and the best to end on), the Swing Version of “Can You Forgive Her?”, a B-side to “Yesterday, When I Was Mad”. And yes, it’s exactly what you think it is, a full big band rendition of the song which is performed excellently - it’s part of a string of acoustic performances tucked away on the singles of this more-computerised-than-ever PSB record: “If Love Were All” features the same band and there’s also the bossa-nova-adjacent Unplugged Mix of “Decadence”. The “Can You Forgive Her?” re-do works stupidly well in my opinion, and the fact that PSB came up with this concept and had it end up this good is just a testament to their genius. So, got all that? Good.

And like their album Please, the Pet Shop Boys followed this album up with Disco 2, another remix album. This album consists primarily of remixes of songs from Very, but there’s a few Behaviour tracks as well. Unlike the first Disco, this disc presented in DJ mix form. Once again, I’d take note of this release as it is related to this album.
