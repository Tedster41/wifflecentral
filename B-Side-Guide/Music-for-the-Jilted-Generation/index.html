B-Side Guide

The Prodigy - Music for the Jilted Generation

While this Prodigy album doesn't have nearly as many B-sides as The Prodigy's first, there's still plenty to look through. The first single from the album, "One Love", released in 1993, had two B-sides, one of which would later appear on the album in a slightly edited form. The single itself would become famous as Liam Howlett, the guy who actually made all the music, had been named as 'the man that killed breakbeat' by many - with the genre declining in 1993, many argued it was Howlett's 'commercialisation' of the genre that destroyed it. To disprove them and show that he wasn't a hack with no talent, he first released "One Love" as a white label under the name "Earthbound", after the name of his studio. The single was heralded by DJs of the time as a fine piece of breakbeat, with Mixmag, who had denounced him and was the leader of the 'prodigy bad' movement, giving it high praise in the underground section of its pages. Howlett must have been beaming like a five-year-old when he saw that. The track was released officially not long after in October, to the dismay of many DJs, learning that they had just proven themselves wrong.

"One Love" was included on the album, but Howlett later said he wished for it to be dropped from the tracklist. Why? Put simply, he ran into the same problem that we have throughout this page. That eighty minute limit for CDs. An edited version of "One Love" had to be included instead of the full length version, leaving the album just two minutes from the limit. If the full length version was used, the album would clock in at 80 minutes and four seconds, over the limit. As well as this, Howlett had to drop another track, "We Eat Rhythm" to fit the album onto the disc. As such, Howlett wanting to drop "One Love" was likely because he preferred "We Eat Rhythm" to go on it, as that would never have a proper release, while most Prodigy fans probably already had the "One Love" single. Now, given this album is in my top ten favourites, I'm not going to mess with it, so I'll leave "One Love" as it is on the album, but put the 12" Mix on the bonus CD.

Alright, but our deal here is finding B-sides. Unfortunately, the bonus disc on later versions of the album, titled More Music for the Jilted Generation, hardly covers the B-sides, so we'll have to work things out ourselves. First, we of course need to include "We Eat Rhythm", which was included on a cassette compilation which came with an issue of Select magazine. Singles "One Love" and "Poison" had two B-sides each, and both had A-sides which varied from the version of the album. The other singles, "No Good (Start the Dance)" and "Voodoo People", had one remix by Howlett each.
