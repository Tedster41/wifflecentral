Blur - Modern Life is Rubbish

…and the award for most British album title goes to…

Blur's first part of their "Life" series was born out of Damon Albarn's frustration with the United States after being forced to do a trudge of a tour in the country. Missing England dearly, he decided the band's new direction for the album was to make distinctly British pop, like that of the Kinks, the Who and other famous British bands with names beginning with 'the'. So, British pop is your idea, eh Damon? Fancy shortening it to 'Britpop'?

Now, in embracing classic pop music, bands like Blur and Oasis also embraced the classic B-side. However, both have done excellent jobs in later years of collecting them, negating my involvement, which is why there's no Oasis albums here. But you may have noticed that the word "Blur" is featured in bold text at the top of the page, and that's because the band have unfortunately slipped up in collecting the B-sides from this album (unlike their superior follow-ups Parklife and The Great Escape). The reason? Like most B-side collections, they generally were limited to only one disc of bonus tracks. But using only four singles, these four gents managed to make enough B-sides to require two CDs to hold them all! And so, here is my own special double disc edition featuring all of the band's B-sides from this period.

To be clear: there were actually only three singles released from this album in particular ("For Tomorrow", "Chemical World" and Ted favourite "Sunday Sunday"), but it was preceded in 1992 with the non-album single "Popscene", which was released to promote the US tour which inspired this album. The song was included in Blur's own B-side disc for this album, so I will do the same. However, the sessions for "Popscene", which were originally intended to create an album, produced a second single, "Never Clever", which was never issued as a single after "Popscene"'s failure in the… well… pop… scene. While a live version of the song was featured as a B-side of the album's second single "Chemical World", the fact that the studio version of "Never Clever" was eventually released on compilation album issued by the band's record label in 1997, to me, justifies the live version's replacement with the original.

Onto the numbers: "Popscene" had four B-sides, "For Tomorrow" had seven (!), "Chemical World" had five, and "Sunday Sunday" had eight (!!). The first three singles will also feature their A-side, because either it was presented in a new version not featured on the album or (in the case of "Popscene") it's just not on the album at all. I hope you can see now why we need two discs. I've distributed the singles evenly, with the first two on the first disc and the second two on the second disc.
