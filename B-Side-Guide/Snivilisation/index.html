Orbital - Snivilisation

After defining a generation with their 1993 eponymous album, Orbital decided to mellow out for the rest of their career. Their first step in this direction was Snivilisation, their 1994 album, and their first one to not be self-titled. Just like the previous album, an EP issued a year away from the album's release featured a remix of a track on this album, this time being 1995's Times Fly, which included "Sad But New", a remix of album track "Sad But True". Therefore, the whole EP will be included on our disc. Alongside this was a single for the track "Are We Here?" from the album, which featured multiple B-side remixes, one of which is actually just four minutes of silence, in protest of the Criminal Justice and Public Order Act of 1994, which effectively banned raves and defined the music played at them as "sounds wholly or predominantly characterised by the emission of a succession of repetitive beats". This act was a major controversy in the dance music scene, for obvious reasons, and led to multiple protests, including ones from musicians like here. Another example of a protest song against this is the track "Their Law", from Music for the Jilted Generation by The Prodigy. In addition, two tracks from the band's 1994 Peel Session which would later appear (in a different form) on this album will also be included.
