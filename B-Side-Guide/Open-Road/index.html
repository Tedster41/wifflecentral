Gary Barlow - Open Road

From the moment his former band Take That broke up, lead singer and sole songwriter Gary Barlow had his eyes on the solo career. He made it quite clear to everyone that he wanted to be free of the boy band and become the next George Michael. Well, to cut a long story short, he failed. His backing vocalist, Robbie Williams, stole his thunder and became one of the best selling artists in the UK. But before Williams could get ahead, Barlow was briefly in the lead with his first solo album, Open Road. Released just over a year after Take That broke up, and months before Williams would release his first album, Barlow's solo debut demonstrated his ambitions, with an album filled with slow ballads, attempting to catch the "Jesus to a Child" and "Father Figure" style that George Michael had set out. The album had two number one singles, "Forever Love" and "Love Won't Wait", the latter being one of the only non-ballads on the record, and my personal favourite. The B-side to the first was "I Miss It All", while the second had "Meaning of a Love Song" and "Cuddly Toy". The third single "So Help Me Girl" had "Ayúdame" (a Spanish version of the A-side), "Million to One", and "Offer My Peace". The fourth single, the title track, was remixed for its release as a single, and had no new B-sides. A month after the release of this single, Williams released his own fourth single from his debut album, the now-legendary song "Angels", which peaked at number 4 in the UK. The "Open Road" single peaked at number 7. Barlow was starting to see the writing on the wall, so he tried a new strategy.

While in Take That, the band's record label had been trying to push them into the American market with their third album, Nobody Else. Their plans fell through, and to this day the band (which is one of the biggest in UK chart history) is regarded as a one-hit wonder stateside, for their hit "Back for Good". Barlow wanted to try again, and repackaged the album for the American market. Four tracks ("Love Won't Wait", "So Help Me Girl", "My Commitment" and "Hang on in There Baby") were remixed, and a new song, "Superhero", was written (with help from Max Martin, who was then just starting out with the Backstreet Boys) and released as a single. And because I'm nice, I'll put this at the start of the disc because it's an A-side rather than a B-side. A third single ("So Help Me Girl" was the first single in the States), the remixed "Hang on in There Baby", now featuring soul singer Rosie Gaines (of "Closer Than Close" fame), was also released in the territory, with a new B-side "Hard to Handle", presented in the form of a live recording (I couldn't find evidence of a stereo version existing). None of these singles drew much success in the states, and the album never even took off enough to flop. Barlow went back home to find Williams dominating the chart, and after a failed follow-up album, would go silent until Take That reformed, without Williams, in 2005. Incidentally, Williams also never managed to crack the US market, which at least means he can live there now and no one will recognise him.

Now, this is everything, but I want to make two more additions which I'd imagine will be slightly controversial given my "no live recordings" (of songs with studio versions available) rule. Included on my disc are live versions of "Back for Good", as included on the American album, and "A Million Love Songs", which was included on the final US single. Now, any Take That fan would realise that these are both Take That singles. "Back for Good" was likely included on the album because it was the only Take That single to find success in the States, and "A Million Love Songs" because it had always been Barlow's "Yesterday", if you follow the analogy. Now, even though Barlow had already performed these songs plenty, and studio versions do exist, I'm including them because the studio versions technically are recorded with someone else, so Barlow's live versions count as new versions ("now with female backing vocalists!"). Plus, they're both good songs, so get off my back, alright?
