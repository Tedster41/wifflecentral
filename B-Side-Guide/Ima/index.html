BT - Ima

BT, or Brian Transeau, is one of the main guys in the trance scene. He at first lived in the trance-starved States, where the genre never really caught on, with house remaining the dominant genre there through the trance-craze of 1999 and 2000. However, Transeau started long before those halcyon days, in 1993, when he released his first single: "Embracing the Future". Already on this first single, BT's special brand of progressive, oceanic trance was on full display. Naturally, it did nothing where he lived. However, over in the UK, there was a certain man with a huge record collection, large enough that overseas records like Transeau's could come into orbit of it - he is the master, the mixing Renaissance, the DJ God Himself warms up for, Sasha! The mixmaster Sasha, who was yet to produce any of his genre-defining DJ mixes, found BT's record and began playing it in his sets. Seeing the producer's potential, Sasha offered to pay for Transeau to move to the UK to pursue his music career. And when someone is offering to pay you to move to a country where people actually appreciate your music, I'd imagine Transeau didn't take long to consider the option.

After moving to the UK, Transeau got to work on his first album, Ima (meaning "now" in Japanese, written as 今). After releasing a number of singles in 1994 and 1995, the album was finished and released in the latter year. Now, usually this is where we'd start assembling the B-sides and all that, but first we must address something critical: This album is a massive mess. Not in the sense that it's bad - it's one of the best albums in the genre - I mean the tracklisting. The CD version, the vinyl version and the US version all have different tracklists and each have songs exclusive to them (kind of). To give you a quick rundown - the CD version has five songs, four of them regular tracks which are also on the vinyl version, but one of them is "Sasha's Voyage of Ima", a 40-minute DJ mix by Sasha of the album's tracks. Transeau said he did the tracklist like this because he "wanted some tracks to stand out for themselves, but [he] also wanted Sasha to show how he interprets songs. He makes every individual record more than just the sum of its parts". Meanwhile the vinyl version features unmixed tracks from both the CD tracklist and "Sasha's Voyage of Ima", plus the bonus track "Poseidon". The US version is a double-disc version, most likely created by the record label when they received two entirely different versions of the album and BT's new non-album single "Blue Skies" and went "fuck it" and just put it all out at once.

Therefore, when making this version, I thought for a while on what the first disc would look like. For some time it was going to be my own custom tracklist merging the formats together, but in the end I decided that artistic intent should take precedent, so combine that with a need for all versions of songs to be accounted for and my solution is to have both the UK CD and vinyl versions present; the US version was the label's intent, not the artist's. You can see this on the right.

Now, with that out the way, let's actually do what we came here for. To start with, we have two bonus tracks. One is "Beta (3AM Mix)", a track which BT released on his website in 2003 and described as being written during the sessions for this album. The second is the much more obvious one, the previously mentioned "Blue Skies", a single with Tori Amos which was released in 1996 and is generally connected to this album. The version which appears on the US album will be used here. Then onto the self-made remixes: there's two BT versions of "Loving You More" and two versions of "Blue Skies". The last thing which I need to mention is the Sasha Remix of "Embracing the Sunshine" - this is the definitive version of this song and was the A-side for its main release, and given its such a major tune in trance history (and Sasha did make that DJ mix for this album), I'm letting it slide. Sasha's other version is too long to fit on the CD though, so we'll have to go without it.
