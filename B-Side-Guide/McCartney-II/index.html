B-Side Guide

Paul McCartney - McCartney II

This is my favourite McCartney album. Not out of all albums that he made, but those in the McCartney series, a series where he plays all instruments. Made in 1979, this album was constructed in sessions at McCartney's home on a sixteen-track machine that he had rented. He made the project simply because he was bored and wanted to make some music, and never actually intended the collection to be an album. It was only when he showed people this double album of content that they said "cut a few tracks and you could put that out". So he did. But McCartney's original tracklist (according to the good folks over at beatlesbible.com) looked a little something like this:

Pretty hefty collection, clocking in at over our CD limit of 80 minutes (horrifying, I know). As previously mentioned, Paul was advised to cut it down a bit, and so he did. Many tracks were dropped, and others were edited. So now, we get to play the game in reverse. With all the cards at the table, we get to ask, where do they get dealt to?

Well, "Secret Friend" and "Check My Machine" would be bound to the B-side realm, "Check My Machine" being edited in the process. "All You Horse Riders", "Blue Sway", "Mr H. Atom", "You Know I'll Get You Baby" and "Bogey Wobble" would be left unreleased, and the remaining tracks would be jigged around (and some edited) until the album felt right. I personally think Paul made some very good choices as to what to drop and what to not, leaving the album with no duds at all (oh and before you ask, yes I'm one of those insane people who likes "Temporary Secretary", maybe I've listened to too much acid techno or something). However, I think "Blue Sway" being dropped was a bad move. It should have either made it to the album as an edited instrumental adjacent (there are some vocals, but they're not exactly verse-chorus) or been the B-side of "Temporary Secretary" instead of "Secret Friend”. Even so, it's not about my opinion, is it?

And as one last mention, the Christmas classic "Wonderful Christmastime" was made in these sessions and released before the album as a non-album single, as Paul cleverly realised that no one would want a Christmas song in the middle of their non-seasonal record, even if the song is an immortal banger.
