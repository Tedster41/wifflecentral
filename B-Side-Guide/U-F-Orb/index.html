The Orb - U.F.Orb

This album is part of a series of articles. Go to the previous article?

Alex Paterson, stood at the sidelines watching his former friend's band explode spectacularly, probably then thought to himself, "…whelp, onto the next album!" U.F.Orb was released in 1992 and debuted at number one on the UK album charts, the only Orb album to grace the top spot. Like its predecessor, it featured long and winding ambient pieces, six of them in total this time (and on one disc!). Strangely enough, the US version of the release was a double album with additional tracks on a second disc, while the US version of the Orb's previous album compressed it down from two discs to one - obviously they wanted to make up for the poor job they did editing it the first time around.

Now, like the previous album, we have quite a few B-sides to get through. To start with, let's focus on the sole single from the album: "Blue Room". In July 1991, a few months after the Orb's debut, it was announced that new chart rules for the UK singles chart were being put into action, which would specifically accommodate dance music singles with their abundance of B-side remixes. Singles could now feature different versions of the same song for up to forty minutes, as opposed to the previous twenty minute limit. Seeing this announcement, the band, who now consisted of just Alex Paterson and Kris Weston, made "Blue Room", a song which lasted thirty-nine minutes and fifty-eight seconds, and released it as a single, which it would now count as. Surprisingly for a band who had previously never even sniffed the arse-end of the top 40, "Blue Room" reached no. 8 on the UK singles chart, and in the process became the longest song to ever enter the charts. To ensure this was the case, the single (like most others from the time) was divided into two discs. One with "Blue Room" as a single track, and the other with some alternate versions. And it's those versions that we care for here! To start with, the full-length original "Blue Room" isn't on the album (or else it would take up half of it), so the forty-minute majesty takes up the first half of the disc. The next two tracks are "Excerpt"s, although they (despite the name) are not excerpts of the full piece, but rather are remixes of it. "Excerpt 605" appeared on that second half of the single I was talking about, while "Excerpt 606" appeared on the US release of the single. "605" has no credited remixer, so it can be assumed it was produced by the same lot that made the original, while "606" is a remix by Orb member Weston. Lastly, we have the "Blue Lamp Mix", an exclusive remix by other Orb member Paterson which was featured on the compilation Taking Liberties, an album put together against the Public Order & Criminal Justice Act 1994, which threatened to ban raves (which it defined as "characterised by the sequential omission of repetitive beats", which definitely isn't the Orb). Put all that together, and we have this:

The rest of the B-sides here aren't as uniform. To start with, we have the other single from the period, although it didn't appear on the album, "Assassin". Released three months after U.F.Orb, the song appeared in two original forms: A-side "The Oasis of Rhythms Mix" and B-side "Chocolate Hills of Bohol Mix". And by the way, the A-side of this single, along with a few versions of "Blue Room", would be the contents of the US's second disc of U.F.Orb. As well as this, we have a B-side to a song from the previous album - "Towers of Dub", which would eventually appear on U.F.Orb, had been around for a while already, being played in Orb gigs like the one documented in Patterns and Textures, the accompanying live set for the band's previous album. And so, the single "Orb in Dub" (released July 1991), which Discogs and Wikipedia define as part of the singles for "Perpetual Dawn" (taken from the Orb's debut), featured that track and "Towers of Dub" on the other side in "Ambient Mix" form. And finally in addition to this, like the Orb's previous set of B-sides, we have a Peel Session, that being a live session on BBC Radio 1 with John Peel, which like the previous pair of Peel Sessions, was released as a little album by the Orb in 1996. The session here is from 1992 and featured a version of "O.O.B.E." and (very strangely) a cover of punk band The Stooges' "No Fun", to which Peel commented "Punk rock, eh? You never really know where you are, do you?" Together, these parts form this:

It's also worth noting that around this time the Orb did a remix single (i.e. they got A-side billing) of Mike Oldfield's "Sentinel", from his 1992 album Tubular Bells II, which featured two long numbers and one 7” edit.

Now, that should be where we stop. But, there are some oddities around this album. A famous bootleg of the Orb's works, titled The Visitor (or sometimes Anthology 2) was released in 1994. Unlike most other bootlegs, which were usually live recordings or pre-released B-sides (like what we're doing), this contained tracks which had never been seen before or since. Now, some of these we're not including here because they're not from the same time period as this album (the first four tracks are from around the time of their first album - the first two tracks I left off my discs for that album because I wasn't too sure on them in general, the third was a demo for what eventually became a System 7 song and the fourth is on CD 6 of my version), but there are some we're interested here.

The first is the "Solaris Mix" of "Towers of Dub", which the bootlegger claims they sourced from an acetate (a special type of record pressing which is usually extremely limited). Any copies of this acetate have never been logged online to what I could find, so I couldn't verify if it was truthfully an Orb remix, but I'll give it the benefit of the doubt here. However, these next two tracks you're going to have to follow me with, because this gets complicated. The following two tracks on the bootleg album are "O.O.B.E." (Pool Mix) and "Blue Room" (Ambient Demo at Mark Angelo's). These two tracks do have a clear source, that being a Mixmag Live! cassette from 1993, where these two (along with two other non-Orb songs) were featured in an Alex Paterson mix. So, the question now is, "Are these just live versions, or are they more special than that"? The "Live" in Mixmag Live! may lead one to think that it's just a live recording that they've put to tape and sent off, and therefore it shouldn't count, but there is evidence to point the other way, primarily in the fact that both of these tracks would see an official Orb release later in life. This version of "O.O.B.E." would be released on the "rarities" second disc of the band's 1998 greatest hits album, U.F.Off (a pun on the title of this album with a NASA-grade middle finger on the cover). Unfortunately for us, that disc is all mixed, and so we only get six minutes of this version, while the The Visitor version is over eleven minutes long. That same mix disc also boasts an "Ambient Mix" of "Blue Room" (as if it wasn't ambient enough), which it says was originally released on The Orb Mixes, although I cannot find any record of such an album. Again since it's in a mix I'm going to have to leave it off here. The other track off The Visitor which is of interest, the "Ambient Demo at Mark Angelo's" of "Blue Room" (which isn't the "Ambient Mix" on U.F.Off, I've checked), was later released on the 2007 deluxe edition of U.F.Orb, which I haven't even got to yet. So considering both versions have appeared on sanctioned Orb releases, and are unique versions that sound like studio recordings (although they could be studio improvisations…), I'm going to let this one slide (also because I have no more time to invest into finding out answers).

And on the note of that deluxe edition, there are tracks on there that I haven't been able to find anywhere else which I'm going to include on this disc. The deluxe edition disc starts off with an Andy Hughes remix of "O.O.B.E.", which considering Andy Hughes was an active force in the Orb (helping out with Live '93 and later joining the group in 1994), I've decided to include. The deluxe edition then continues to include the Ambient Mix of "Towers of Dub", which was on our previous disc, the aforementioned Ambient Demo of "Blue Room", and then mixes of "Close Encounters" and "Majestic" which we haven't seen before. The new mix of "Close Encounters" is listed as "Ambient Mix 1", while "Majestic" is simply "Mix 1". Now while I have had grievances with deluxe edition bonus discs released years after the original (I'm looking at you Fatboy Slim), I'm going to let these go through since I have no evidence against them. The final track on that disc is the B-side version of "Assassin", which we've already covered, although I find it strange they didn't include the A-side version, since it isn't represented in the deluxe edition. Either way, our disc is pretty much full now, and good thing too, because I'm tired of looking through bootleg releases on Discogs. But, here is the fruit of my labour:
