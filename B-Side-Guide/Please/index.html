Pet Shop Boys - Please

How the Pet Shop Boys got into the charts is a bit of a strange tale. They started in New York in 1983, working with producer Bobby Orlando. Singer Neil Tennant had met him while in New York interviewing Sting for Smash Hits, a UK magazine which he wrote for. Orlando produced a number of songs for the duo, which would later appear on their first and second albums - but not Orlando's versions. Their first single, Orlando's version of "West End Girls", did alright in the States for the duo, and so they followed it up with "One More Chance". With the evidence of a hit on their hands, they ran back home and signed with Parlophone, where they worked on their first album, Please, with producer Stephen Hague. They also made a version of "Opportunities" with different producers which was released as a single - the track was re-recorded for the album with Hague behind the mixing desk. Then with the album produced by late 1985, "West End Girls" was once again released as the lead single, this time being Hague's version. This version appeared in the UK charts at the modest position of 80, but over the course of two months crept up to the number one spot, and now suddenly people wanted to buy the album. Three more singles followed, "Love Comes Quickly", Hague's "Opportunities", and a (fucking fantastic) remix of "Suburbia" done by Julian Mendelsohn. All of these singles had one B-side each, apart from "One More Chance" (which had none) and "Suburbia" (which had two).

So, let's start with the B-sides, which I've put at the end of the album.

Now let's move onto the remixes. First, we'll start with the early versions of tracks before Stephen Hague began producing for the duo. This includes "One More Chance", despite its eventual release being on the follow-up album Actually, just because I'm going to prioritise the date over the album it ended up being released on. Every main single had a 12" mix which will be included here, apart from "Opportunities", as the 12" mix for it was a remix by Shep Pettibone. "West End Girls" also has a special 10" single release with its own mix, plus the original Bobby Orlando version of its B-side "A Man Could Get Arrested", which was released in 1985. There are three other tracks, the Disco Mix of "Love Comes Quickly" B-side "That's My Impression", the Italian remix of "Suburbia" B-side "Paninaro" which was, as the title suggests, exclusively released as a single in Italy (and then worldwide on the Disco compilation - we'll get to that), and the Original New York Mix of album track "Why Don't We Live Together?", which was released later by the Pet Shop Boys in their Further Listening series.

One other thing that should be noted is that there was a remix album put out later in 1986 to accompany the album, titled Disco. It contained six songs, two of them B-sides and four of them album tracks, presented in a remixed form by various producers. The single version of "Suburbia" and Italian remix of "Paninaro" are also present on here, but it's worth mentioning as an accompanying disc to this set. And on a personal note, the version of "In the Night" on there has got to be the definitive version.
