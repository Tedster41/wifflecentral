The Orb - The Orb's Adventures Beyond the Ultraworld

This album is part of a series of articles. Go to the previous article?

Work on The Orb's first album, The Orb's Adventures Beyond the Ultraworld, began in 1990. Alex Paterson, now joined with his friends Andy Falconer and Kris Weston as the Orb after his former friend Jimmy Cauty left (and allegedly took the band's tapes with him), worked on the album with some of his other mates, such as Youth, who in a few years would work with Paul McCartney. In April 1991, the album they had been working on was released as a double album, with close to two hours of ambient music. Its sources stretch far and wide, and the final product reflects this with its sprawling songs, attempting to reflect travelling through space. It takes an acquired taste to really enjoy the album, that or some carefully aquired… substances. It had three singles, "A Huge Ever Growing Pulsating Brain That Rules from the Centre of the Ultraworld" (that's a mouthful), which was released and recorded when Cauty was still a member, then after he left, "Little Fluffy Clouds" and "Perpetual Dawn". All of these singles were released before the album. Following the album, there were some other releases worth mentioning. The first is the Aubrey Mixes: The Ultraworld Excursions remix album, featuring new versions of tracks from the album. Allegedly, this was intended to be the third disc of Adventures Beyond the Ultraworld, but considering this was The Orb's first album, and thus they hadn't proven themselves yet, their record label Big Life wasn't about to let them put out a triple album. This album was released on 2 December 1991, and was at first released and deleted on that day, so the only stock available was what had been bought by record stores for the release day. It was later re-released in 1994 when Big Life were in financial troubles and were trying their best to make ends meet by reissuing old properties. And look, while a couple remixes on here could be counted as external, the majority was made by Paterson and his best buds who helped him with the album, so I'm having the whole thing be the second third disc (it's a double album, remember!).

Right, now let's move away from pre-assembled discs and work on our own ones. Let's discuss the B-side remixes and bonus releases of the Orb. First, we have the Orb's two appearances on the compilation Eternity Project One in 1989, "Tripping on Sunshine" (Live Mix) and "Borrowed Love" (The KLF Slow the Beats), with the latter included because Jimmy Cauty, a member of the KLF, was also still a member of the Orb at this point. Then we have the Kiss EP, consisting of four tracks. After that, of course, is the "A Huge Ever Growing Pulsating Brain that Rules from the Centre of the Ultraworld" single, based on a Peel session (20 minute live broadcasts on John Peel's show on BBC Radio 1) which the band had. It was after this that Cauty left the band, and the following tracks and remixes were done without him. First, the re-release of the above song (no I'm not typing it out again) which featured the new "Orbital Dance Mix" (not to be confused with the band Orbital who were gaining traction at the time - this mix was done by the Orb themselves) of the song. Later that year, the band had another Peel session which featured remixes of two songs from the upcoming album, the second of which has a completely normal, typical, ordinary ending. Don't worry about it. Both Peel sessions were released on the 1991 Orb Peel Sessions album. After their second Peel session were the "Little Fluffy Clouds" and "Perpetual Dawn" singles, which both featured remixes of the title track and remixes of songs on the album. And one other thing, an alternate version of "Supernova at the End of the Universe", titled "Reefer Spin in the Galaxy", was featured on a Volume compilation (a compilation filled with exclusive songs from various artists) in September 1991. Now, all these combined do not all fit onto one disc, so we must split them. The first disc of the two consists of songs made with Cauty…

Well, it would be Paterson, had it not been for the fact that Cauty was in another band.
